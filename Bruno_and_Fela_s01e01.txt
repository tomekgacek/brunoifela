# Bruno & Fela – Episode 1: The First Day at Forest School

[Narrator]

Far away, beyond the hills covered in ferns, there was a magical place called the Whispering Leaves Forest.

It was no ordinary forest. Enormous oak trees stretched their branches almost all the way to the clouds. Soft, moss-covered paths wound between the trees, and every morning, a delicate mist floated above the ground like fluffy cotton candy. Birds sang cheerful songs, streams whispered over smooth stones, and the leaves rustled gently whenever the wind passed through the branches.

All sorts of animals lived there. Foxes, hedgehogs, martens, sparrows, badgers, squirrels, and many more called the forest their home.

And right in the heart of the forest stood the oldest tree of them all: the Great Oak. It was so enormous that seven bears, holding paws, would have been needed to wrap their arms around its trunk.

And right beneath this magnificent tree stood Forest School.

Today was a very special day.

It was the first day of school!

[Fela]

Bruno! Bruno! Wake up!

[Narrator]

But Bruno, a little beaver with a very comfortable bed, had no intention of getting up just yet. He pulled his blanket over his head and mumbled that he needed five more minutes.

[Fela]

But we don't have five more minutes! It's the first day of school!

[Narrator]

Bruno suddenly opened his eyes. School! He jumped out of bed so quickly that he got tangled in his own blanket and landed in a soft heap on the floor.

That was certainly a very scientific way to begin the day.

[Fela]

Excellent work, Bruno. A perfect demonstration of gravity!

[Narrator]

Bruno sat up and brushed a little fluff from his nose. Being a beaver, he liked order, plans, and lists. Lots and lots of lists. Sometimes he even made lists of things he needed to add to other lists.

Fela, on the other hand, was a little squirrel who loved running, jumping, and climbing trees. She usually did things first and thought about whether they were a good idea afterward.

Bruno reached for his backpack and proudly announced that he was ready. Fela asked whether he had made a list for his first day of school.

Of course he had! In fact, he had made three lists.

Fela's eyes widened. Three lists? How many things were on them?

Seventy-two, Bruno replied proudly.

Seventy-two?! Fela wondered whether they were going to school or climbing a mountain.

Bruno explained that it was always better to be prepared. Then he opened his backpack and began checking his supplies. His snack was there. His notebook was there. His pencil was there. His spare pencil was there, too. And, of course, he had packed a spare spare pencil.

Fela stared at him in disbelief. Why on earth did he need three pencils?

Bruno thought for a moment and replied that it was a little like asking why a woodpecker needed so much pecking.

[Fela]

That explains absolutely nothing!

[Narrator]

And with that, the two friends set off toward Forest School.

The morning sun peeked through the leaves, painting golden spots on the path. Birds sang overhead, and a little stream bubbled merrily nearby. The closer Bruno and Fela got to the Great Oak, the more little animals they met, all heading in the same direction.

Along the way, they met a young hedgehog named Jasper. He was carrying a tiny backpack and walking rather slowly.

Jasper asked whether they were going to school, too.

[Fela]

Yes! Today's the big day!

[Narrator]

Jasper admitted that he was feeling a little nervous. Bruno confessed that he was nervous as well, although he tried to hide it behind a very serious expression.

Fela quickly announced that she wasn't nervous at all.

She sounded so confident that anyone would have believed her. Anyone, that is, except for the little butterflies fluttering around in her tummy. There seemed to be an entire butterfly parade in there!

After a few moments, Fela quietly admitted that perhaps she was just a tiny bit nervous.

And that was perfectly normal.

Whenever we try something new, we sometimes feel a little worried. Even grown-ups feel that way. Starting at a new school, meeting new friends, or entering an unfamiliar place can make our tummies feel funny. But sometimes, the things that frighten us at first turn out to be wonderful adventures.

After a few more minutes of walking, the friends rounded a bend in the forest path.

And suddenly, everyone stopped.

[Fela]

Wooooow!

[Narrator]

There it was: the Great Oak!

Up close, it was even more impressive than it had looked from a distance. Its enormous trunk resembled a little wooden house, and sturdy wooden steps wound between its giant roots. A swing made from strong forest vines hung from one of the lower branches. Several levels above, tiny balconies peeked out between the leaves, while wooden bridges connected one branch to another. Colorful flags fluttered proudly at the very top.

Fela could hardly believe her eyes. Was this a treehouse?

Bruno studied the enormous structure carefully and decided that it was probably three treehouses. Jasper thought it might be five, but he had stopped counting.

Fela declared that it was the most beautiful school she had ever seen.

Bruno gently pointed out that it was also the only school she had ever seen.

[Fela]

Well, that's true, too!

[Narrator]

Just then, a warm voice greeted the little animals.

Standing by the entrance was their teacher, Mrs. Owl. She wore round glasses, had kind eyes, and spoke in a gentle voice that made everyone feel as though everything was going to be all right.

She welcomed all the new pupils and explained that they would spend the day getting to know one another and discovering all the wonderful things they would learn together.

Then she invited everyone inside.

The animals climbed the broad wooden steps and reached a spacious platform nestled among the branches. Above the entrance hung a colorful sign made from leaves and tiny twigs.

CLASS 1B – LITTLE EXPLORERS.

[Fela]

Little Explorers! I love that name!

[Narrator]

Bruno agreed. It sounded much better than something like Class Number Seven.

Mrs. Owl smiled and explained that there were no numbers in her classroom. There were only friends.

Inside, several little animals were already waiting.

There was Leo the Fox, who could hardly sit still for even a moment. There was Clara the Marten, who asked so many questions that sometimes she couldn't keep up with her own answers. There was Milo the Bear Cub, the biggest animal in the class but also one of the calmest. And there was Frankie the Sparrow, who spoke so quickly that his words sometimes seemed to bump into one another.

Especially when he was excited.

And Frankie was almost always excited.

He fluttered straight over to Bruno and Fela and began talking before they had even put down their backpacks. He wanted to know whether it was their first day, too. Then he told them that he had heard they might plant acorns. Or perhaps they would do it tomorrow. Or the day after tomorrow. Or maybe they would plant something else entirely!

[Fela]

Does he always talk that fast?

[Narrator]

Leo the Fox grinned and replied that Frankie was actually speaking quite slowly today.

Everyone giggled, including Frankie, who seemed delighted that he had managed to entertain his new classmates before lessons had even begun.

A moment later, Mrs. Owl clapped her wings to get everyone's attention.

She had their first little activity ready. Each pupil was to share one interesting thing about themselves.

Leo proudly announced that he could jump over three bushes in a single leap. Clara explained that she knew the names of seventeen different kinds of mushrooms. Milo admitted that he could fall asleep almost anywhere.

[Fela]

Is that really a talent?

[Narrator]

Milo nodded very seriously. He explained that it was extremely useful, especially after a big lunch.

Everyone laughed again.

Then it was Bruno's turn. He explained that he could recognize most trees by looking at their bark. Mrs. Owl pointed toward the window and asked him to identify the tree outside.

Bruno examined it carefully and announced that it was an oak.

Mrs. Owl chuckled. That one had been rather easy!

Next came Fela's turn. She proudly announced that she could climb almost any tree in the forest.

Leo immediately asked whether she meant every single tree.

[Fela]

Almost every tree!

[Narrator]

Fela explained that once she had tried climbing a tree trunk that didn't have any branches at all.

Leo wanted to know what had happened next.

[Fela]

I learned the meaning of the word "almost"!

[Narrator]

The whole class burst into laughter, and even Mrs. Owl had to adjust her glasses because she was smiling so much.

After the introductions, Mrs. Owl asked everyone to sit in a circle so they could begin their first classroom activity.

All the little animals found a place on the soft cushions.

All except Fela.

[Fela]

Ouch!

[Narrator]

Bruno immediately turned around and asked what had happened.

Fela had sat down on a pinecone!

She jumped up, rubbed her tail, and inspected the cushion as though it had betrayed her.

[Fela]

My very first school lesson: always check what you're sitting on!

[Narrator]

Mrs. Owl nodded thoughtfully and declared that it was a very valuable lesson indeed. Bruno even considered adding it to one of his seventy-two list items.

But before he could find a pencil, the lesson continued.

The pupils drew pictures of their homes and told their classmates about their favorite games. Mrs. Owl showed them the school library, where the air smelled of wood and old books. There were shelves filled with stories about brave explorers, clever animals, mysterious caves, and distant lands.

Bruno discovered a wonderful book about trees. Then he found another one. And another three!

Fela, meanwhile, had discovered something much more exciting: a branch outside that looked absolutely perfect for climbing.

The teacher also showed them the school garden, where wild strawberries grew beside fresh mint and colorful forest flowers. The animals explored little paths, peeked behind bushes, and discovered all sorts of interesting corners around the Great Oak.

Leo was already organizing a climbing competition. Clara was asking Mrs. Owl another dozen questions. Frankie was telling everyone a story about an acorn that had fallen into the stream.

Then he told another story.

And then a third.

It was becoming rather difficult to keep track.

And something else was happening, too.

Time seemed to be flying by.

At least, that was how it felt to Bruno and Fela. Only a little while earlier, they had been standing nervously outside the school, wondering whether they would fit in and whether everything would go well.

Now they were laughing, exploring, and talking to their new friends as though they had known them for ages.

Before they knew it, Mrs. Owl gently clapped her wings once more.

She announced that their first school day had come to an end and that it was time to head home.

[Fela]

What?! Already?

[Narrator]

Bruno was just as surprised. Surely they had only arrived a few minutes ago!

Mrs. Owl smiled and explained that this was often what happened when you had a really good day. Time seemed to pass much faster when you were busy learning, playing, and having fun.

She wished everyone a lovely afternoon and reminded them that tomorrow would bring another day full of discoveries.

Bruno and Fela waved goodbye to their new classmates and started along the forest path toward home.

The sun was slowly sinking behind the trees, painting the clouds in shades of gold and pink. Birds were returning to their nests, and the forest was growing quieter.

Fela noticed that the butterflies that had fluttered around in her tummy that morning had disappeared completely.

She walked beside Bruno for a while before speaking.

[Fela]

You know what, Bruno? This morning, I thought school was going to be scary.

[Narrator]

Bruno asked her what she thought of it now.

[Fela]

It was actually pretty fun! Apart from the pinecone.

[Narrator]

Bruno nodded thoughtfully. The pinecone, he agreed, had been a rather demanding teacher.

He suggested that perhaps the pinecone should be invited back for another lesson tomorrow.

[Fela]

Hey! Don't even think about it!

[Narrator]

Fela laughed so loudly that her giggles echoed between the trees. Bruno joined in, and soon both friends were laughing as they continued down the winding forest path.

After a little while, Fela asked whether Bruno thought tomorrow would be just as much fun.

Bruno considered this very carefully. He couldn't guarantee anything, he explained, but if Fela remembered to check her seat before sitting down, the chances were looking very good.

[Fela]

Bruno!

[Narrator]

And off they went, laughing all the way home through the Whispering Leaves Forest.

Sometimes, the things we worry about most are the things we haven't tried yet. A new place can seem strange, a new adventure can feel frightening, and meeting new friends can make our hearts beat a little faster.

But once we take that first step, we may discover that things aren't nearly as scary as we imagined.

Sometimes, we even discover something wonderful.

And that was exactly what Bruno and Fela learned on their very first day at Forest School.

New places might seem a little frightening at first, but they can soon become the beginning of the most amazing adventures.

And who knows what tomorrow will bring?

Perhaps a new discovery. Perhaps a new friend. Or perhaps another very uncomfortable pinecone!

Whatever happened next, Bruno and Fela were ready to find out.

[Narrator]

End of Episode One: The First Day at Forest School.
